# 2. Series foundation, world, format, and project structure

## Premise and educational approach

Construction + adventure + age-appropriate life lessons. A recurring group of children follows real construction professionals as they solve understandable construction problems. The stories should work as stories, rather than tours filled with lectures. Construction vocabulary is real and explained through action, dialogue, and illustration.

Working reader audience: **ages 4–8**. Character age: approximately **8**. Book 1 format is **32-page full-color illustrated picture book**. The current production target is **Amazon KDP paperback**, with a locked **8.5 × 8.5-inch finished trim per page** and **full bleed**. Facing story pages are developed primarily as continuous two-page landscape illustrations, then laid out for print. Exact final export/template dimensions, cover/spine calculations, ISBN/imprint, pricing, and other platform-production details remain separate final-production tasks and should be checked against the current KDP template before print export.

Tone: warm, curious, expressive, collaborative, believable, and child-friendly. Characters speak as children, not miniature licensed professionals. No one child is always right. Each book combines a real construction lesson with an emotional lesson such as patience, teamwork, asking questions, checking work, or pride in doing a job correctly.

Recurring rhythm: **Curiosity → problem → different ideas → investigation → discovery → professional resolution → construction lesson → emotional payoff.**

The five perspectives are planning, engineering, field operations, design for people, and safety. Tradespeople receive visible credit for actually constructing the project.

## World and access

The children share Mrs. Harper's third-grade classroom. School topics, community developments, STEM activities, and approved field trips can launch stories. Mrs. Harper is a recurring bridge to the construction world; she does not have to appear at every future visit. In Book 1 she brings the group to the site.

Every project has five books:

2. Structure and exterior.
3. Systems behind the walls.
4. Finishes, testing, and inspections.
5. Completion, turnover, and opening day.

# 4. Series-wide character interaction and story behavior

The strongest solutions come from combining different perspectives rather than making one child consistently correct. Character dialogue should sound like intelligent children, not miniature licensed professionals.

The adults respect the kids' curiosity without giving them unrealistic professional authority. The children may ask questions, observe work, read simplified drawings, help with safe measurements under supervision, build models, compare options, spot understandable discrepancies, suggest ideas, and identify safety concerns. They do not operate heavy machinery, perform hazardous construction work, work at heights, handle energized systems, or make final professional decisions.

Recurring construction professionals and trade specialists should be credited visibly for doing the actual work. The series should continue reinforcing that successful construction depends on planners, designers, engineers, superintendents, safety professionals, skilled trades, inspectors, owners/users, and community stakeholders working together.

### Recurring specialist depiction rule

When a construction scene materially shows work from a trade that already has a named recurring specialist, use that specialist as the recognizable trade lead whenever applicable, even if the specialist has no dialogue. Generic workers may appear as supporting crew members, but they should not replace the established named specialist for that trade. If a trade does not yet have a locked specialist, approved fictional background workers may be used until a recurring specialist is established.

Mrs. Harper can launch stories through school lessons, STEM activities, community developments, career discussions, field trips, math, science, art, and local history. Mayor Whitaker appears only when a completed facility is ready to function as part of Middle Creek.

## Page 31
# Little Specs Construction Facts

### What is a foundation?
A foundation supports a building and helps transfer its weight safely into the ground.

### What does a surveyor do?
Surveyors use special tools and careful measurements to determine exactly where things should be built.

### What is rebar?
Rebar is reinforcing steel placed inside concrete to help make it stronger.

### Why do pipes sometimes go under the floor?
Some pipes and conduits must be installed before concrete covers the area.

### Why do construction workers put stickers on hard hats?
Project stickers can represent jobs, companies, training, milestones, or places a worker has been.

The Little Specs received their **Fire Station project sticker during safety orientation**, just like project stickers are often handed out at real jobsites.

### Vinny’s Safety Corner
Construction sites can be dangerous.

**Never enter an active construction site unless you are with an authorized adult and following the site’s safety rules.**
